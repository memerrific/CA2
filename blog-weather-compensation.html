Weather compensation and heating curves explained.

A well-set heating system does not need the same water temperature on a mild autumn day as it does in the coldest part of winter.

What weather compensation does

An outdoor temperature sensor or weather data allows the controller to vary the heating flow temperature. As outdoor temperature falls, the system raises the target flow temperature; as conditions become milder, it lowers it.

What is a heating curve?

The curve describes how strongly the target water temperature responds to outdoor temperature. A curve set too high can cause unnecessary cycling and higher losses. A curve set too low may leave the coldest rooms short of heat.

Why steady operation can be better

Lower-temperature systems often perform best when they supply only the heat the building needs, for longer periods, rather than repeatedly delivering short bursts of very hot water.

Commissioning still matters

Weather compensation cannot correct poor hydraulic balance, inadequate emitters or unsuitable controls. The curve should be tuned alongside flow rates, room performance and customer preferences.
