Boiler conversions

Will a combi boiler improve your water pressure?

Usually, the honest answer is: not by itself. A combination boiler can only work with the water available to it.

Pressure and flow are not the same

Static pressure is measured when no water is moving. Dynamic pressure is measured while water is flowing. Flow rate is the volume available over time. A property can show reasonable static pressure but still deliver disappointing flow when several outlets operate.

What changes after a conversion?

A conventional or system arrangement may use stored hot water and a shower pump. A combi heats mains water as it is used, so the incoming main, internal pipework, boiler output and distance to the outlet all affect the experience.

What should be checked first?

  • Incoming static and dynamic pressure
  • Available flow rate at realistic demand
  • Number of bathrooms and likely simultaneous use
  • Existing shower pumps and stored-water equipment
  • Pipe routes and hot-water delivery time

If the supply is inadequate, a correctly designed accumulator or boosted-water arrangement may be considered separately. It should never be assumed that changing the boiler alone will solve the problem.
