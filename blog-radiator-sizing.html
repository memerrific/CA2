Radiators · Heat pumps

Do radiators need changing for a heat pump?

Sometimes—but replacing every radiator without calculating the room demand is guesswork.

Lower temperatures change radiator output

A radiator produces less heat when the water flowing through it is cooler. Heat pumps normally perform best at lower flow temperatures than a traditional boiler, so each room needs enough emitter output at the proposed design temperature.

Start with room-by-room heat loss

The calculation should account for the size and construction of each room, insulation, glazing, ventilation and the chosen internal temperature. Existing radiators can then be checked against that demand rather than judged by appearance alone.

Some radiators may stay

Oversized existing radiators can often remain. Other rooms may need a larger panel radiator, an additional emitter or underfloor heating. Pipe sizing and available flow also matter; a larger radiator cannot correct restricted distribution.

Design for the whole system

The aim is the lowest practical flow temperature that still heats every room on the design day. That supports comfort, efficiency and sensible running costs.
